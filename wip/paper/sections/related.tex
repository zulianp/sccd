\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

CCD is conventionally split into two stages. A \emph{broad phase} is a
conservative filter: it discards pairs of primitives whose swept bounding
volumes cannot meet. A \emph{narrow phase} then decides each surviving pair
precisely enough to be trusted. The two stages have separate literatures and
separate failure modes.

\subsection{Broad phase}

The simplest broad phase tests every pair. It is trivially parallel and
trivially correct, because the only operation on a box is a floating-point
comparison, and comparison is exact. It is also quadratic, which makes it
unusable past a few tens of thousands of primitives.

\emph{Sweep and prune}~\citep{baraff1992dynamic,cohen1995icollide} sorts the
boxes along one axis and walks a window of overlapping intervals, which reduces
the expected cost to $O(n\log n)$ plus the number of overlaps. Parallel and
incremental variants are
numerous~\citep{capannini2018adaptive,tracy2009sweep,liu2010realtime}, and the
method suits modern hardware because it needs only a parallel sort followed by
independent comparisons.

\emph{Uniform grids and spatial hashes}~\citep{tang2018icloth,tang2018pscc} bin
each box into the cells it touches and test only within a cell. They parallelise
well and need no sort, and they introduce a parameter, the cell size, which the
literature repeatedly identifies as the method's weak point. A cell that is too
small lets duplicate candidates exhaust memory, and one that is too large
degenerates towards brute force. No single value serves a scene whose geometry
changes over the simulation. Published heuristics tie the cell to the average
edge length and the average displacement, which is a reasonable prior and not a
guarantee. \Cref{sec:bp:cell} derives the cell from the swept boxes themselves
and caps the cell count relative to the element count, so no value is left for a
user to choose. For one list against itself these methods also have to suppress
the pairs a box meets in more than one cell, whether by a rule on the cell or by
a pass over the output. \Cref{sec:bp:self} gives a box one cell and reads
forward from it, the arrangement linked-cell neighbour search
uses~\citep{allen2017liquids}, which requires the cells to carry an order and a
bound and requires no test on the pairs.

\emph{Hierarchies} of bounding volumes, KD-trees and interval
trees~\citep{serpa2019kdtree,serpa2020broadmark,zomorodian2000boxes} prune
better per test. They are also harder to build and refit in parallel; on a GPU
their traversal is irregular and their construction wants dynamic allocation.

\citet{belgrod2025toi} benchmark thirteen broad-phase implementations against
exact ground truth, and find that several of them \emph{miss collisions}. The
usual cause is a fixed-size candidate buffer that discards overflow silently.
They conclude that sweeping along the most-varying axis is the most effective
GPU strategy, and propose \emph{Sweep and Tiniest Queue}, which vectorises the
sweep and balances work through a consumer queue. Our broad phase bins the
boxes, so it inherits neither the sort's cost nor its dependence on one axis
separating the geometry.

\subsection{Narrow phase}

For linear trajectories and no minimum separation, a vertex-face or edge-edge
query reduces to finding roots of a cubic. The oldest family of methods solves
that cubic numerically: find a time of coplanarity, then test whether the
contact point lies inside the primitive~\citep{provot1997collision,
bridson2002robust}. These methods assume exact arithmetic. In floating point
they both miss collisions and invent them, and nothing the method controls
bounds the error.

\emph{Conservative advancement}~\citep{mirtich1996impulse,tang2010local}
advances time by repeated minimum-distance queries, and
\citet{lan2022penetration} give a recent GPU variant. It is fast, and with a
large enough safety factor it is usually safe. Distance computation involves
square roots, however, so the underestimate it relies on is not certified.
Tightening the factor to obtain a sharper time of impact reintroduces missed
collisions~\citep{belgrod2025toi}.

\emph{Exact} methods exist in two kinds, and neither answers the question this
paper asks. Root-parity tests~\citep{brochu2012efficient, tang2014bernstein,
wang2022rootparity} cannot distinguish zero roots from two, which is
insufficient for CCD. Symbolic root finding is genuinely exact, takes seconds
per query, and produced the ground truth used in this paper; it is not a runtime
method.

\emph{Inclusion-based} root finding is the family this work belongs to. These
methods bracket the root: they evaluate an inclusion function over a box in
parameter space, discard the box when the function's range provably excludes the
origin, and otherwise refine~\citep{snyder1992interval, snyder1993interval,
vonherzen1990geometric}. Interval arithmetic~\citep{moore2009interval} gives
such a bracket, but it overestimates. \citet{wang2021tight} construct an
inclusion function specific to these queries, together with a forward error
bound. Theirs is the first implementation whose conservativeness survives the
implementation, and TightInclusion is that implementation.
\citet{belgrod2025toi} restructure the same mathematics for a GPU. They
parallelise over interval splits and alternate two global queues, and they
observe that a direct CUDA port of TightInclusion does not scale, because of
branching and register pressure.

Two further implementations answer neighbouring questions and are measured in
\cref{sec:results:others}. Scalable CCD returns one earliest time of impact for
the whole step, which lets it prune every box against a running bound. Additive
CCD~\citep{li2021codim} advances a conservative bound until the primitives
touch, which is cheap per pair and stops well short of the root, and it is the
narrow phase the codimensional IPC line of work uses.

SCCD inherits TightInclusion's mathematics. It uses the same inclusion function
in the same expression order, the same certified bound and the same acceptance
conditions, and it differs only in how the search is realised on each processor.
\Cref{sec:np:order} gives the argument that licenses the difference, and
\cref{sec:parallel} gives the two realisations. We do not consider a search that
is part this one and part the reference: such a hybrid inherits the certificate
of neither, and the reference itself serves a caller who wants the reference's
answer.
