\begin{abstract}
Continuous collision detection (CCD) decides whether two meshes moving along
linear trajectories touch during a time step, and when. A simulator that
guarantees an intersection-free state can tolerate neither a missed collision
nor a late time of impact: either lets the solver step through the contact. We
present SCCD, a CCD pipeline for triangle and quad meshes, conservative by
construction on both CPU and GPU, assessed against the certified reference
implementation TightInclusion over $11{,}630{,}064$ checks against exactly
known roots.

The broad phase is a two-dimensional cell list with no sort and no user-chosen
cell size, with a query per pair type: vertex-face bins each box into every cell
it touches, and edge-edge, being one list against itself, binds each box to the
one cell holding its minimum corner and walks forward from it. The host
narrow phase packs SIMD lanes with boxes from \emph{different} queries, which
an order-independence argument shows is legal. The device narrow phase pairs a
small per-block stack with a double-buffered global queue, so a query needing
millions of boxes spreads across the machine.

Over six scenes and $6{,}394$ cases on one \textsc{GH200} node, SCCD reports no
missed collision and no late time of impact. On the host, median and
worst-case error match TightInclusion's on all twelve scene-phases to every
printed digit, at $1.7\times$ to $4.9\times$ its speed. The device is
$2.5\times$ to $26.6\times$ faster and agrees to within $3\%$ and $4.5\%$. Its narrow phase nevertheless runs at $0.37\times$ host speed on the smallest
scene, which is enough to put the whole pipeline behind the host there.
\end{abstract}
